# 06 · Content Checklist

**This is the thing most likely to stall the project.** Design and code are the fast part;
writing is slow. Everything here goes into `docs/CONTENT.md`, and §1–5 are launch-blocking.

You can start Phases 0, 1 and 3 without writing a word. Phase 4 needs this done.

---

## 1 · Identity — 30 min

- [ ] Name as you want it displayed
- [ ] **Headline** — one line, what you do. "Backend engineer building data-heavy systems", not "passionate developer"
- [ ] **Tagline** — two sentences for the hero
- [ ] Location
- [ ] Open to work? (drives a visible badge)
- [ ] Social links: GitHub, LinkedIn, Stack Overflow, Medium, X
- [ ] Contact email to display

> Your current GitHub profile says "passionate frontend developer" — worth deciding whether that's
> still how you want to be positioned, given the backend-heavy stack you just chose.

## 2 · About — 1 hour

Three paragraphs, written the way you talk. Not a résumé.

- [ ] **What you do and what you're good at** — the specific thing, not "full stack"
- [ ] **How you got here** — the short version of your path
- [ ] **Who you are outside work** — this is what makes people remember you

## 3 · Skills — 30 min

Every technology you'd be comfortable defending in an interview.

- [ ] Grouped: Backend · Frontend · Cloud & DevOps · Data · Tools
- [ ] Each rated 1–5. **Be honest with the 5s** — a 5 means you'd be the person others ask
- [ ] Optional per skill: years used, a project that used it

## 4 · Experience — 1 hour

Per role: company, title, dates, and **three bullets of achievements with numbers**.

- [ ] Not duties. "Cut API p95 from 800ms to 120ms" beats "worked on APIs"
- [ ] If you don't have the number, estimate honestly and say so — "roughly halved"
- [ ] Tech stack per role
- [ ] Company logo if you can get a clean one

## 5 · Projects — 3 to 6 · 2–3 hours

**The centrepiece.** Use the [project model](./02-data-model.md#the-project-model) as your
questionnaire. Per project, at minimum:

- [ ] Title, slug, one-line summary
- [ ] Kind (professional / personal / open-source / academic) and status
- [ ] Timeline label — "2025 · 4 months", free text
- [ ] **Problem** — what was broken, and who it hurt
- [ ] **Approach** — what you built and why that way
- [ ] **Outcomes** — at least one before/after metric
- [ ] Tech stack, with the primary ones marked
- [ ] Cover image + screenshots
- [ ] Links: live, repo, docs — whatever exists

Then optionally, for the ones you care most about: architecture description and diagram,
challenges, learnings, future work, testimonial, awards, collaborators.

### Two notes

**Work projects count.** Set `client.confidential: true` and the client's name never reaches the
browser — the page renders "a logistics client" instead. You can describe what you built and what it
achieved without naming anyone.

**College and learning projects count too**, especially with an honest "what I'd do differently now".
That reads as self-awareness, which is more persuasive than a polished project with no reflection.

## 6 · Assets

- [ ] Résumé PDF (current)
- [ ] Headshot — decent lighting, neutral background
- [ ] Project screenshots — 1600×900, real UI, not mockups
- [ ] Favicon
- [ ] Company / tech logos where you have rights to use them

## 7 · Later — not launch-blocking

- [ ] Education
- [ ] Certifications
- [ ] Blog posts (Phase 8)
- [ ] Testimonials

---

## Tips

**Write badly first.** Get all five sections down in one sitting without editing. Editing prose
that exists is easy; editing a blank page is not.

**Specific beats impressive.** "Built a Django service that reconciles 40k daily transactions"
tells a reader far more than "extensive backend experience".

**Cut every instance of "passionate", "hardworking" and "team player".** Everyone writes them, so
they carry no information. Show it in the project write-ups instead.

**The projects are what get read.** Most visitors scan the hero, skim the projects, and leave. Put
your effort there, in that order.
